How is Speed Measured in the Platform?

📄 Version 1.0 👤 Audience: Technical Support / Installation Team

The platform identifies Assets that are over the speed limit by using the lower of: map road speed limits, Zone speed limits (if set), and Asset Event Triggers (Max Speed and Tolerance) to generate speeding events. The platform applies the lower limit, with the tolerance added on, as the working speed limit.

The platform does not know what type of vehicle you are tracking — it could be a Caterpillar Grader, a 10-ton Truck, or a high-performance car. Each vehicle type has different maximum speeds allowed, and map road speed limits may not apply equally to all of them.

1. How the Speed Limit is Determined

The platform contains map sets with road speed limits for individual roads, and combines these with Zone limits and Asset Event Trigger settings to calculate the working speed limit for each position.

Example: a Caterpillar Grader may be configured with a Max Speed of 30 and 0 Tolerance — it would be flagged as speeding at any speed above 31, regardless of whether the road speed limit is 40, 50, or higher. Any road speed limit above the configured Max Speed (30) is ignored in this case.

2. Accuracy & Limitations

There are a number of limitations when using individual road speed limits. Where possible, the platform errs on the side of caution to prevent excessive speeding events — it uses the higher speed limit when there is any uncertainty, and temporarily suspends speed checking when no data is available.

Three factors need to be taken into account regarding accuracy:

Data Accuracy

Road speed data is provided by GPS navigation mapping companies and is usually updated bi-annually. This data is available for most countries, but may be missing in some. Key Telematics cannot guarantee access to road speed data for any specific country, nor guarantee the accuracy of such data.

GPS Position Accuracy

Modern GPS receivers can have inaccuracies in recorded co-ordinates (based on factors like installation and canyon effects). This means determining which road a vehicle is travelling on is usually a best-guess effort. The platform accounts for this by considering surrounding roads when determining the local speed limit.

Example: if a road within 100 meters of the current position has a higher speed limit than the road the platform believes the asset is on, the platform uses the higher speed limit instead of the closest road’s limit — this eliminates issues caused by GPS inaccuracies.

Position Logging Interval

The road speed limit feature is platform-based, meaning the platform uses position data sent by the Device to determine speeding. The platform can only use the positions the Device sends it, and cannot know what speed the vehicle reached in between those data positions. Accuracy is therefore determined by the update frequency of the Device.

Recommendation: an update frequency of at least every 15 seconds and 15-degree turn gives the best results, without incurring unnecessary data costs.
